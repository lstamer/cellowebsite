### How to choose your wedding ceremony music (especially for solo cello in Cape Town)

Choosing ceremony music becomes much easier when you stop hunting for a single “perfect song” and start thinking in terms of **moments**. Most ceremonies have four musical anchors, and each one needs music that does a different job:

## 1. Prelude – music that settles the room

**What it does:**

- Welcomes guests as they arrive, greet one another, and find seats
- Creates atmosphere without demanding attention
- Lets the room feel like the ceremony has already begun in spirit

**What it should sound like:**

- Warm, lyrical, and calm
- Not too dramatic too soon
- Supports conversation and gentle anticipation

**Good options for solo cello:**

- Classical: simple movements from Bach, Handel, or Vivaldi; hymn melodies; gentle baroque or romantic pieces
- Film themes: lyrical, flowing themes that sit well on a single melodic instrument
- Contemporary: tasteful, stripped-back arrangements of meaningful songs that work melodically without lyrics

Think of prelude music as **setting the tone rather than making a statement**.

## 2. Processional – the emotional hinge of the ceremony

**What it does:**

- Frames the emotional entrance, whether it’s one partner, both partners, or a full wedding party
- Focuses the room’s attention on the aisle and the moment
