## Body

If you’re comparing musicians for a wedding, private function, or corporate event, the smartest thing you can do is ask better questions.

Beautiful photos and polished social clips only show that someone looks good online. They do not tell you how that musician will behave under pressure, adapt to timing changes, or fit into the actual flow of your event.

These seven questions will tell you far more.

## 1. What kind of training and performance background do you have?

Event performance is not just about knowing a few popular arrangements. Training shapes tone, musical judgment, consistency, and the ability to respond in the moment.

You are not necessarily looking for a long biography. You are listening for whether the musician has a real foundation behind the performance they are offering.

## 2. Can I hear a live sample, not just a polished studio clip?

Studio recordings are useful, but live-event work happens in real rooms, with real variables. A live sample gives you a better sense of sound, presence, and musical control in context.

It also tells you whether the performance quality holds up outside a curated recording session.

## 3. Do you have testimonials or references from real clients?

Professionalism is experienced by other people long before it becomes visible on your day.

A strong testimonial can reveal whether the musician was communicative, punctual, adaptable, and calming to work with, not just whether the music sounded good.

## 4. Do you provide your own PA if the venue needs amplification?

Many clients do not know whether their venue or event actually requires a PA. A professional should be able to advise on that and, where appropriate, provide a simple solution.

This question helps you understand whether the musician is prepared for practical event conditions rather than assuming everything will be ideal.

## 5. How do you handle timing changes on the day?

Timings change constantly. Ceremonies start late, speeches run over, guests move slowly, and coordinators reorder things at the last minute.

A good event musician should already expect this. You want someone who can adapt without making the change feel stressful or noticeable.

## 6. What happens if you are ill or there is an emergency?

It is not the most glamorous question, but it is an important one.

Professional musicians should have a clear answer about contingency planning. That might mean a trusted deputy network, a formal replacement process, or another practical backup plan. The point is not to assume disaster. The point is to know there is a plan.

## 7. How do you help clients choose the right music?

This may be the most revealing question of all.

A musician who only waits for instructions is very different from one who can guide you. You want to know whether they can help with ceremony structure, repertoire suggestions, venue realities, timing, and what will actually work for your event.

## What you are really listening for

Across all seven questions, you are trying to understand three things:

- musical quality
- professionalism
- judgment

Most people focus only on the first. In event work, the second and third matter just as much.

## Final thought

The best musicians do not only sound good. They make the entire process feel easier.

Ask questions that reveal how someone works, not just how they market themselves, and you will make a much more confident booking decision.
