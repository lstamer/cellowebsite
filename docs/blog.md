### Post 1

**Title:** How to Choose the Right Music for Your Wedding Ceremony (A Cape Town Cellist's Guide)

**Slug:** wedding-ceremony-music-guide-cape-town

**Brief:**
Walk couples through the structure of a wedding ceremony (prelude, processional, register, recessional) and what musical choices work best at each stage. Include examples from classical and contemporary repertoire, explain how to incorporate a meaningful personal song, and address the common mistake of choosing music based on what sounds impressive rather than what fits the moment. Ideal reader: couple in the planning phase who has no idea where to start with ceremony music.

**Target word count:** 900–1,200 words

---

### Post 2

**Title:** Do You Actually Need Live Music at Your Corporate Event? (An Honest Assessment)

**Slug:** live-music-corporate-events-worth-it

**Brief:**
Cut through the cliché. Not every corporate event needs live music, and this post should say that honestly — then explain the specific conditions where it makes a real difference (client entertainment, award evenings, brand activations, year-end functions with a meaningful budget). Include what to look for in a performer, what questions to ask before booking, and what "professional" actually means in a live music context. Ideal reader: event manager or PA doing research before committing.

**Target word count:** 800–1,100 words

---

### Post 3

**Title:** 7 Questions to Ask Before Booking a Cellist for Your Cape Town Event

**Slug:** questions-to-ask-before-booking-cellist

**Brief:**
Practical checklist-style post that doubles as a buyer's guide and an implicit case for why Luke ticks every box. Questions should include: What qualifications do you hold? Can you provide references or testimonials? Do you carry your own PA if needed? How do you handle last-minute timing changes? What happens if you're ill on the day? Can I hear a recording of a live performance? Each question should have a 2–3 sentence explanation of why it matters. Ideal reader: anyone in the final stages of vetting musicians.

**Target word count:** 700–1,000 words
