# Stamer Cello — Showreel Production Pack

**Working title:** "Handled."
**Runtime:** 90 seconds master (plus 60s and 30s cutdowns)
**Audience:** event planners, coordinators, venue owners/managers, restaurant & hotel F&B managers, wedding planners, corporate event agencies
**Format:** 16:9 master at 4K; 9:16 vertical re-cut for WhatsApp/Instagram sends
**Tone:** premium editorial, calm, warm, characterful. Never hype. Ebony green / cream / coral accent, Cormorant Garamond titles.

---

## 1. The strategic idea

You're not selling "a cellist". A planner already knows what a cellist is. You're selling **one less thing to manage**. The belief ladder from the site applies here, in this order:

1. *This is premium, and right for my events.* (look, venues, sound)
2. *People like me use him and vouch for him.* (planner/venue testimonials, not couples)
3. *He's effortless and reliable.* (setup, timing, logistics, zero-fuss)
4. *He plays beautifully.* (the floor, not the pitch — but it must be heard)

So the film's spine is **a single event day told from the planner's point of view**: Luke arrives, sets up himself, reads the room, plays, packs down, and the planner never had to think about him. Music carries it. Luke's voice appears in short, dry, confident lines — the site's "rebellious-classical" voice — and the testimonials come from people *in the B2B audience's own job*.

The one line they should remember: **Effortless live music, handled.**

### Voice approach: hybrid
Luke on camera for **one** opening line and **one** closing line (eye contact sells the person), plus voiceover for 3–4 lines over footage. On-camera pieces are shot in a venue, cello in frame, not a talking-head studio.

### Alt lines (pick the ones that sound like you when read aloud)

Opening:
- "You've got three hundred moving parts. I'm the one that shows up tuned."
- "Every event has a list. I'm the line you can tick before you've started."

Setup VO:
- "I don't need a stage, a sound engineer, or a green room. I need a chair and a plug. Sometimes not even the plug."

Range VO:
- "Classically trained. Happily unconventional. I'll play the Bach. I'll also play the thing your guests will Shazam."

Closing:
- "One less thing. Let's talk."
- "Send me the date. I'll handle the rest."

**Read-aloud rule:** every line should be speakable in one breath, with a dry half-smile. If a line makes you sound like an ad, cut it.

## 3. Shot list

Shoot everything at 4K, 25fps base with 50fps for anything you'll want to slow down (bow close-ups, walking, guest reactions). Log profile, expose for skin. Keep white balance consistent within each venue so the three "rooms" feel distinct but the film feels unified.

### A. Staged shoot day (the backbone — one venue, one long afternoon into blue hour)
Book a venue that already fits the brand: a Winelands estate terrace, a design-forward restaurant, or a hotel with a beautiful lobby. Ask them for it free in exchange for the footage and a credit — that's your first venue relationship right there.

| # | Shot | Lens / movement | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| A1 | Macro: bow hair on string, first note | 85–100mm macro, locked | The opening frame. Shoot 6+ takes, nail the hit. |
| A2 | Wide: empty venue, Luke crosses with case | 24–35mm, locked, tripod | Golden hour. Shoot both directions of travel. |
| A3 | Luke on camera, opening line | 50mm, f/2, slight push-in on slider | Seated with cello. Eye-line just off lens or straight down lens — test both. 8+ takes. |
| A4 | Luke on camera, closing line → plays last phrase | 50mm, same setup, blue hour | Venue now lit/dressed behind him. |
| A5 | Arrival detail set: car boot, case latch, amp, cable coil, chair placed, tuning peg, rosin | 50mm + macro, handheld on gimbal | 10–15 clips of 3–5s each. Shoot at 50fps. |
| A6 | Venue manager points, Luke nods, thumbs-up sound check | 35mm, handheld | Use the real venue manager. Unscripted, natural. |
| A7 | Slider across cello at bow height, mid-performance | 50–85mm, motorised slider | Hero beauty shot. Shoot in golden and blue hour. |
| A8 | Over-the-shoulder from behind Luke, looking out at the room | 24mm | Establishes "performer's view". Works empty and full. |
| A9 | Low angle from the floor, bow arm silhouetted against window | 35mm | Editorial. Backlit. |
| A10 | Hands only: left hand on fingerboard, vibrato | Macro, 50fps | Cut on musical phrases. |
| A11 | Pack-down: case closes, handshake with planner, walk out | 35mm, gimbal follow | Get a clean handshake — this is the "handled" image. |
| A12 | Luke walking through door into a full/glowing room | 24mm, gimbal, from behind | Only if you can dress a few extras/guests for blue hour. |
| A13 | Static plates of the venue: table settings, glassware, candles, florals | 85mm | Cutaways and end-card backgrounds. |

### B. Real event footage (gather across the next 4–8 bookings)
Ask every client *in advance* for filming permission (one line in your booking confirmation). Bring the camera on a tripod at the back; a second body or phone on a small tripod at the cello.

| # | Shot | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| B1 | Wedding: guests arriving with Luke visible playing in background | Wide, tripod. The most important "real" shot. |
| B2 | Wedding: ceremony aisle moment with cello in frame | Only with couple's blessing. Even 3s is gold. |
| B3 | Corporate: drinks reception, branded environment, Luke playing amplified, people chatting | Shows the "fits the room" claim. Avoid logos you don't have permission to show. |
| B4 | Restaurant: dinner service, warm low light, cello near the bar | Shoot at f/1.4–f/2, high ISO; grain is fine. |
| B5 | Guest reactions: someone turning to look, a phone raised to film, applause | Candid. Long lens from the back. |
| B6 | Dance floor / upbeat set (if you do one) | Proves range. |
| B7 | Planner / venue staff interacting with Luke, run-sheet in hand | The "I just put him on the run sheet" visual. |
| B8 | Any drone establishing of a venue (if permitted) | 3s max in the film, but great for openers on the cutdowns. |

### C. Testimonials (2 in the master, shoot 4 to choose from)
Target people whose *job title* matches the viewer: a wedding planner, a venue/events manager, a corporate event agency producer, a restaurant GM. Couples are lovely but not this film's audience.

- Shoot at their venue, in natural light, 50mm f/2, them slightly off-centre, eye-line just off lens.
- Two-camera if possible (wide + tight) so you can cut mid-sentence.
- Lav + a shotgun backup. Record 60s of room tone.
- Don't give them a script. Ask these questions and let them talk; you'll cut the one sentence:
  1. "What did you have to do to manage Luke on the day?" (you want: "nothing")
  2. "What did guests say?"
  3. "Would you put him on the run sheet again? Why?"
  4. "Finish this sentence: 'Booking Luke means…'"
- Get their name, role, and venue spelled correctly on camera at the end.

## 4. Things to say, and things not to say

**Say** — specific, operational, planner-language: *run sheet, set-up time, own PA, no rider, early, amplified or acoustic, repertoire on request, 15 minutes, one contact.*

**Don't say** — anything from the site's banned list: *elevate, seamless, unleash, unforgettable, magical, bespoke, next level, elevate the atmosphere.* No superlatives about your own playing. Let the testimonials and the sound do that.

**Supers** should be nouns and numbers, not adjectives. "Own PA · 15 min setup · No rider" beats "Stress-free professional service".

## 5. Sound

Music is the product, so the soundtrack must be *you*, recorded properly — not the on-set audio.

1. **Studio/clean recording first.** Record the full 90s arrangement in a dry room with a good condenser pair (or book a studio for two hours). Aim for one continuous piece with a clear structure: sparse intro (0:00–0:14) → builds through setup (0:17) → lyrical in performance block 1 → a stylistic turn or two in block 2 (this is where a pop/jazz arrangement can appear) → peak at the proof strip (1:07) → a resolving final phrase with natural decay (1:27).
2. **Shoot performance footage to playback** of that recording so the bowing matches the edit. Use a click-less count-in and a visible clap for sync.
3. **Keep room tone and diegetic sound** under the montage: case latches, the chair scrape, the cable, a glass set down. These sell "real".
4. **Dialogue:** lav on Luke for on-camera lines; VO recorded in a treated room, close mic, low and unhurried. Leave air around lines.
5. Mix for phone speakers first; planners will watch this on WhatsApp. Check it in mono.

**Rights:** an original arrangement of public-domain repertoire (Bach, Elgar, Saint-Saëns) is safe. A pop cover in a video you send commercially is a sync-licence problem — keep any pop moment under a few seconds or use a public-domain piece that *sounds* contemporary. Do not underscore with a Spotify track.

## 8. Pre-production checklist

- [ ] Lock the 90s music arrangement and record it clean (this gates everything else)
- [ ] Secure the staged-shoot venue (pitch: free footage + credit, they become your first "preferred supplier" venue)
- [ ] Book two testimonial subjects with B2B job titles; schedule at their venues
- [ ] Add a filming-permission line to your booking confirmation email for upcoming events
- [ ] Gimbal, slider, macro lens, lav + shotgun, two bodies if possible
- [ ] Wardrobe: one look for the staged day (dark, tailored, no logos); match it across the real events where you can so the film reads as one man, one standard
- [ ] Print the shot list; tick on the day
- [ ] Cut a rough assembly to the clean music *before* grading so the rhythm is right first
- [ ] Grade: warm, slightly desaturated highlights, deep greens in shadow — should sit next to the website without a seam
- [ ] Captions burned in for the 9:16 versions (sound-off viewing)
- [ ] Three end cards, QR tested on a phone

## 9. Send strategy (so the film actually gets events)

The film is the attachment; the message is the hook. A first WhatsApp or email to a planner should be two lines and the 30s or 15s cut, not the master:

> Hi [Name] — I'm a Cape Town event cellist; planners tend to book me because I'm the supplier they don't have to manage. 30 seconds of what that looks like: [link]. If it's useful, I'll send a rate card for [venue/their next event type].

Send the full 90s only when they reply. Track which end-card version went to whom so you learn which ICP converts.
