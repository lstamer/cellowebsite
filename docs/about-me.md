# About Me — Page Copy

---

## Page Headline

The cellist behind the music

**Subheading:**
I've been playing cello since I was seven years old. Twenty-something years later, I'm still just as in love with it — and I've found that the best place to share that love is at the moments that matter most to people.

---

## Section 1 — The Beginning

**Headline:** It started before I could fully reach the strings.

**Body:**

I was seven years old when a cello first appeared in my life. I don't remember choosing it — I think it chose me. There was something about the sound: warm, deep, human in a way that I couldn't explain then and still find hard to put into words now.

From that first lesson, I was hooked. I practised obsessively, competed wherever I could, and slowly began to understand that the cello wasn't just an instrument I played — it was the primary way I made sense of the world.

---

## Section 2 — The Training

**Headline:** Serious training at the highest level.

**Body:**

I'm currently studying at the University of Cape Town under the guidance of Graham du Plessis — one of South Africa's foremost cellists. The training at UCT doesn't let you cut corners. Every technique, every phrase, every interpretive choice is examined and refined.

That discipline shapes everything I bring to a performance. When I play at your event, you're hearing years of structured, rigorous training — not someone who picked up a cello and learnt a few songs.

**Highlights:**
- University of Cape Town — studying under Graham du Plessis
- Competitor in the Hubert van der Spuy Competition
- Winner of the Concerto Festival
- Soloist at the Cape Town City Hall
- ATCL qualification

---

## Section 3 — The Stage

**Headline:** Competition stages taught me something rehearsal rooms couldn't.

**Body:**

Competing sharpens you. When you walk onto a concert stage — the Cape Town City Hall, a competition final, a festival showcase — there's nowhere to hide. The music either lands or it doesn't.

Performing as a soloist at the Cape Town City Hall was a moment that changed how I understood my own playing. The acoustic, the audience, the weight of the occasion — it demanded everything. I walked off the stage knowing something had shifted.

Those high-pressure experiences are exactly why I'm calm and in control at your event, no matter what happens on the day.

---

## Section 4 — Why I Play at Events

**Headline:** Classical training meets real life.

**Body:**

Concert halls are extraordinary, but they're not where most people encounter live music. Events are. Weddings. Celebrations. Farewells. Corporate evenings that the team has looked forward to all year.

These are the moments when music does something that a playlist never can — it marks the occasion. It tells everyone in the room that this moment matters.

I play at events because I've seen what happens when the music is exactly right. Guests go quiet. Conversations pause. Someone tears up without quite knowing why. The atmosphere lifts.

That's what I'm here to create for your event.
