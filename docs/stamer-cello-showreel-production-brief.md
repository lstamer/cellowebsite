# Stamer Cello Showreel Production Brief

## Purpose

Create a short proof-led film that makes a prospective client believe four things, in this order:

1. Luke fits the kind of event I am planning.
2. Real people trust and respond to him.
3. He handles the music and logistics properly.
4. He sounds excellent.

The film should feel calm, warm and real. It should not feel like a generic luxury wedding advert or a music video built from instrument close-ups.

## Core idea

**The cello belongs in the room when something matters.**

This gives the film an emotional belief while the footage supplies practical proof.

## Deliverables

Create these from the same production:

1. **Main showreel:** 70 to 74 seconds, 16:9, for the website, proposals and planner outreach.
2. **Wedding reel:** 30 seconds, 9:16, built specifically for couples and planners.
3. **Corporate and private-event reel:** 30 seconds, 9:16.
4. **Three short hooks:** 12 to 15 seconds each, 9:16.
5. **Captioned versions:** Burned-in subtitles for every spoken edit.
6. **Clean versions:** No captions, for future recuts.
7. **WhatsApp delivery file:** H.264 MP4 under 25 MB.

Do not make the vertical versions by blindly cropping the horizontal master. Record important shots in both orientations, or compose the 4K image with enough centre-safe space.

## Final spoken script

### Direct to camera

> I don't think the cello belongs locked in a concert hall. It belongs in the room when something matters.

### Voiceover

> I'm Luke. I play weddings, private events and corporate functions around Cape Town. Bach, film scores, Sinatra, Taylor Swift. Same instrument. Very different rooms.

> Before I play a note, I've learned the songs that matter, planned the timing with your venue, and brought the gear the room needs.

> So when the doors open, the speeches end, or the room finally lets go, you're not wondering what's happening with the music. You're in the moment.

### Direct to camera

> If you've got a date in mind, send me the date, venue and what you're planning on WhatsApp. I'll take it from there.

The spoken script is intentionally short. The music and client proof need room.

If Luke does not want to name artists, replace that sentence with:

> Classical music, film scores, jazz, modern pop. Same instrument. Very different rooms.

## Performance direction for Luke

- Speak to one person, not an audience.
- Keep the delivery quiet and conversational.
- Look directly into the lens.
- Leave a small breath after the first sentence and before the final CTA.
- Do not memorise the whole script as one speech. Record it in the four sections above.
- Record three usable versions of every section:
  - calm and intimate
  - slightly warmer
  - firmer and more direct
- Avoid a presenter voice, broad hand gestures and an exaggerated smile.
- Begin and end each take with three seconds of silence while holding the same posture.

## Music plan

Use two contrasting passages, not a rapid medley.

### Passage one: emotional and spacious

- A ceremony-friendly phrase with a clear melodic resolution.
- Seven to ten uninterrupted seconds in the final edit.
- It should demonstrate tone, control and emotional restraint.

### Passage two: familiar and unexpected

- A modern, film, pop or jazz arrangement that guests visibly recognise.
- Seven to nine uninterrupted seconds in the final edit.
- It should demonstrate the modern side of the instrument and a change in room energy.

Record both pieces in full, even though the edit only uses short passages. A complete performance gives the editor natural entry points, resolutions and future social clips.

### Rights plan

- For the website and direct business use, use music that is public domain, original, commissioned or properly cleared for promotional synchronisation.
- Luke's own recording of a public-domain composition is the simplest safe foundation.
- A modern composition, backing track and arrangement may each carry separate rights.
- Platform music permissions do not automatically cover the website, paid advertising or files sent directly to prospects.
- Confirm clearance before the final edit is published.

## Controlled shoot

Plan one half-day controlled shoot at a real event venue. It should look like an event space, not a studio pretending to be one.

### Direct-to-camera coverage

- Full script, horizontal medium close-up
- Full script, vertical medium close-up
- Opening line in three delivery styles
- Final CTA in three delivery styles
- Wedding-specific CTA: "If you've got a wedding date in mind, send me the date, venue and the parts of the day you're planning music for. I'll take it from there."
- Corporate-specific CTA: "If you've got an event date and run sheet, send me the venue, timings and what the room needs. I'll take it from there."
- Ten seconds of Luke looking naturally towards camera without speaking for edit flexibility

### Performance coverage

For each of the two chosen pieces, record:

- One complete locked wide take
- One complete medium three-quarter take
- One complete tight portrait take
- Bow-hand detail
- Left-hand detail
- Cello body and microphone detail
- A slow, controlled side move
- A vertical medium performance take
- A vertical wide or full-body take

Do not stop after obtaining a few eight-second fragments. Full takes protect continuity and audio synchronisation.

### Process coverage

- Arriving with cello and compact gear
- Opening the case
- Rosining the bow
- Tuning
- Checking a real run sheet
- Reviewing a setlist
- Attaching the clip-on microphone
- Connecting to Luke's speaker
- Connecting to a venue PA with a technician
- Testing backing-track level
- Moving the chair into position
- A short discussion with a planner or venue coordinator
- Spare strings, second bow and backup microphone visible briefly
- Quiet load-out after the room has emptied

## Real-event footage required

A credible reel cannot come from the controlled shoot alone.

### Wedding

Capture at least:

- Luke playing as guests arrive
- Processional or another emotionally legible ceremony moment
- Couple reaction
- Parent or guest reaction
- Wide shot showing Luke's position relative to the ceremony
- Cocktail-hour performance
- Guests recognising or discussing a song
- Planner or coordinator checking timing with Luke
- One clean exterior or venue-establishing shot

### Private event

Capture at least:

- Luke playing near a dinner table, garden gathering or milestone celebration
- Guests listening rather than generic decor
- A quiet, intimate musical moment
- A lighter moment where the room relaxes
- Host interaction before or after the performance

### Corporate function

Capture at least:

- Luke in a clearly corporate room, with approved branding or programme context
- Arrival or drinks reception
- Run-sheet or AV coordination
- Soundcheck with the venue technician
- Performance under event lighting
- Audience response
- A clean stage or room wide

If a category is unavailable, do not fake it. Build the neutral master from the audiences for which real proof exists, then update it as the library grows.

## Client or planner interview

Record the interview immediately after the event in a quiet area. Keep the camera close enough for clean sound. Ask all three questions and let the editor choose the most natural answer.

1. What were you most uncertain about before booking live music?
2. What was easiest about working with Luke?
3. What did you notice in the room when he played?

Do not ask the client to repeat a prepared sentence. A slightly imperfect real answer is more persuasive than polished praise.

## Audio capture

Audio quality is non-negotiable.

Record:

- Close cello microphone on an isolated track
- Backing track on an isolated track, if used
- Stereo room ambience on separate tracks
- Luke's spoken voice on a lavalier or boom microphone
- Camera scratch audio for synchronisation
- At least 30 seconds of room tone in each location
- Natural applause, laughter and guest reactions

Recommended recording format: 48 kHz, 24-bit WAV.

The final mix should combine the close cello sound with enough room ambience to prove that the performance is live. Do not place pristine studio audio over obviously live footage unless the treatment is intentionally disclosed and visually coherent.

## Camera capture

- Main footage: 4K, 25 fps, 1/50 shutter.
- Selected slow motion: 4K, 50 fps, 1/100 shutter.
- Lock white balance and exposure. Do not use automatic shifts during a take.
- Capture real-time footage as the foundation. Use slow motion sparingly.
- Keep camera movement controlled. One gentle move is enough.
- Frame Luke's face and full playing posture more often than isolated hands.
- Compose critical shots for both 16:9 and 9:16.
- Record each important reaction for at least six seconds without whipping away.

## Visual direction

### Wardrobe

- Wedding and outdoor material: Luke's cream suit with dark shirt or another approved cream-and-dark combination.
- Corporate material: dark tailored look, agreed with the event dress code.
- Avoid changing wardrobe so often that the reel feels like unrelated stock footage.

### Colour and light

- Natural skin tones.
- Cream, ink and deep green should carry the visual world.
- Keep coral to one restrained graphic accent in titles or the end card.
- Use a lightly muted grade rather than full saturation or full monochrome.
- Avoid the heavy orange wedding-film grade and strong teal shadows.

### Composition

- Photography and video remain sharp rectangular frames.
- Use Cape Town or the Winelands once as a location cue, not as a tourism montage.
- Human behaviour should identify the event type.
- Avoid repeated macro shots of strings, cufflinks, champagne and flower details.
- Avoid drone footage before the viewer has seen Luke.

## Edit rules

- Begin with Luke already playing.
- Show Luke's face or full playing posture within the first second.
- Show a genuine reaction within five seconds.
- Never begin with a logo.
- Cut on musical phrases and human responses.
- Let two musical phrases play for seven to ten seconds each.
- Use natural room sound as punctuation.
- Keep transitions mostly invisible. Straight cuts and restrained dissolves are enough.
- Use no more than three short text proof moments.
- Keep the end card to one action: WhatsApp.
- Subtitle every spoken word in the social versions.
- Do not use generic claims such as "world-class", "unforgettable" or "perfect for every occasion".

## Text graphics

Use text only where it adds proof or orientation.

Possible cards:

- `WEDDINGS · PRIVATE EVENTS · CORPORATE FUNCTIONS`
- `12+ YEARS PLAYING`
- `NEVER MISSED AN EVENT`
- `CAPE TOWN`

End card:

> Luke Stamer  
> Cape Town cellist  
> Tell me about your date on WhatsApp

Optional qualification test for a separate version:

> Performances from R4,500

Do not include the complete package ladder in the reel.

## Permissions and proof

Before publication, confirm:

- Client and guest appearance permissions
- Venue and planner permission
- Photographer and videographer licences
- Permission to show corporate names or event screens
- Exact testimonial wording and attribution
- Composition, arrangement, backing-track and recording rights
- No unapproved claim about insurance

Keep a simple release log containing asset filename, event, creator, visible people, permission status and approved usage.

## Production sequence

1. Choose the two musical passages and confirm rights.
2. Secure one controlled venue and one real wedding capture.
3. Confirm release language with the wedding client, planner, venue and videographer.
4. Record the controlled shoot and direct-to-camera sections.
5. Capture the real event and client interview.
6. Assemble the 70 to 74 second rough cut without graphics.
7. Review story, music continuity, proof and spoken naturalness.
8. Add restrained titles, captions, grade and final audio mix.
9. Export the master and purpose-built cutdowns.
10. Test the main reel in the website and WhatsApp contexts before publication.

## Approval checklist

The reel is ready only if every answer is yes:

- Is Luke unmistakably visible within one second?
- Is there a genuine human response within five seconds?
- Does the viewer hear at least two satisfying musical phrases?
- Are wedding, private and corporate contexts real and legible?
- Does the film show preparation and logistics, not only performance?
- Does someone other than Luke substantiate the promise?
- Is every claim, face, venue and musical work approved for use?
- Is there one clear WhatsApp next step?
- Does the film still work with captions and the sound muted?
- Does the full version feel complete by 74 seconds?
